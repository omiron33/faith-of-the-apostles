# The Faith of the Apostles: director brief

An emotional powerhouse of belief: the faith carried through the dark. The film is its own
cinematography, illustrating the lyrics, every frame drawn in code.

- **Choruses, the Trisagion, the Theotokos verse**: the Church of the Savior on Spilled Blood at
  night, every surface a code-drawn mosaic or marble, candlelight and chandeliers; the camera looks
  up into the dome on "Holy God".
- **The struggle**: a Gothic cathedral in darkness, moonlight through the clerestory in shafts,
  one bank of votive candles the only warmth; lightning through the windows.
- **The verses** each have their own world: the old road at dusk, the Sea of Galilee with twelve
  lanterns kindling, the map of the Apostles' journeys lit with lines of fire, a moonlit ruin
  crossed by a lantern's light, apostolic succession as a row of tapers each lit from the last,
  two shores at night drifting apart, cracked earth splitting in 1054, light descending on a dark
  sea, the empty tomb, the mended cup, iron glowing in the forge (theosis).
- **The filioque** is an outlandish error to be rejected, never something beautiful: a cheap
  magenta neon sign planted crooked in the cathedral's nave, buzzing, garish; on the echo lightning
  strikes, it dies and topples, and the Creed's own words stand: "who proceeds from the Father"
  (John 15:26; Constantinople, 381; nothing added).
- **Words** are the star: big, sharp, one reading path, at most two lines, each word on its sung
  onset and set by what it means (lib/type.js): the faith words heavy and lantern-amber, God in
  bone-white capitals with warmth, division in cold steel, mercy a rose-white italic cry, the dark
  words rain-grey, the filioque alone in its loud magenta face.
